Zone 2: The North Star Metric

Our Primary Performance Compass & Guardrails

Primary Core Metric

Total Activities Logged

The direct expression of core product utility. If a user uploads an activity, they successfully worked out and synced telemetry data.

It drives social density, feeds the feed algorithm, and directly leads to habit retention and paywall exposures.

Why not MRR?

Revenue is a lagging business outcome. Optimizing it directly encourages short-term paywalls that kill the free network base and choke the long-term acquisition funnel.

Constellation Safeguards

Risk Mitigation & Counter-Metrics

1. Metric Gaming (Goodhart's Law):
Risk: Users uploading multiple fake/empty 1-minute manual activities to gamify streaks.
Mitigation: Track GPS-Verified Activity Rate (%) and Median Active Session Duration.
2. Extreme Climate Seasonality:
Risk: Activities plunge in winter, making short-term performance look like failure.
Mitigation: Analyze trends on a 12-Week Rolling Average and evaluate YoY cohort curves.
3. Power-User Skew:
Risk: Top 5% athletes logging 10 activities/week mask overall user base churn.
Mitigation: Balance as a Constellation with Weekly Active Loggers (WAL).
NSM Caveats Notes: - Total Activities Logged is our primary star, but it must be paired with guardrails (Constellation). - Goodhart's law states that when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure. By tracking GPS-verified activities and median duration, we prevent gamification. - Tracking Weekly Active Loggers (WAL) ensures we monitor the breadth of active users, not just volume skewed by hyperactive users.

Zone 3: Squad Level

Mandate, Risks, and Core Metrics

Squad Mandate

Strava Activation Squad

What breaks if we disappear?
The cold start problem remains unsolved. Acquisition budgets (CAC) are burnt as new users download the app, see an empty feed, fail to pair device sensors, and churn.

The top of our active user funnel becomes a leaking bucket.

Target Metrics: First-week engagement density, sensor sync, social setup.
Main Squad Metric

Activation Rate

% of new users who log ≥ 1 activity AND follow ≥ 1 friend in their first 7 days.
Why this dual-criteria approach?

Strava is defined by the intersection of utility tracking and social community.

A user who logs activities alone gets no kudos or comments, leading to a 50% higher churn rate. A user who connects socially but logs no telemetry remains a passive zombie.

True "activation" requires both facets.

Squad Mandate Notes: - Clearly frame the Cold Start problem: it represents the single biggest point of user leakage. - Defend the dual-aspect of our Activation Rate definition: product tracking and social connection. - If we only optimized for the first run, we'd fail to capture the network effects that actually keep users from switching to Apple or Garmin native apps.

Zone 4: Signals & Guardrails

Success Metrics vs. Failure Risks

Quality & Success Signals HEART Framework
Adoption:
Challenge Opt-in Rate (% of onboarding cohorts tapping 'Accept').
Engagement:
Active Streak Progression (Median consecutive active days).
Task Success:
Day-7 Completion Rate (% of opt-ins receiving the profile badge).
Expansion / Referral:
Social Share CTR (% of completers posting badges to IG/Feed).
Trade-offs & Mitigations Guardrails
⚠️ Goodhart's Law (Metric Manipulation):
Risk: Users logging multiple empty 30-second workouts manually to secure the badge.
Mitigation: Track **% of Streak Workouts logged via Manual Entry** (vs GPS). Set a 5-minute threshold filter for manual logs.
⚠️ Notification Burnout:
Risk: Seven days of push nudges irritate users, triggering global push opt-outs.
Mitigation: Track **14-Day Push Opt-out Rate (%)** of challenge participants vs control.
⚠️ The Post-Challenge Cliff:
Risk: Sudden drop in logging on Day 8 as extrinsic gamified goals vanish.
Mitigation: Track **Day 8-14 Retention Rate (%)** of completers. Trigger a Day-7 post-challenge club recommendation to establish intrinsic social roots.
Success and Risks Notes: - Highlight the Post-Challenge Cliff: This is a classic behavioral psychology trap where extrinsic rewards (the badge) can crowd out intrinsic motivation (enjoying the workout). - Our mitigation is to redirect users on Day 7 to local running/cycling clubs, transitioning them from a solo gamified challenge to community-based motivation.

Presentation Strategy

Securing the +20% Presentation Bonus (Attack & Defense Guide)

🛡️ Defending Our Tree & Feature
Q: "Why is Activities Logged your NSM and not active subscriber volume?"
Defend: "Paid subscribers are a lagging output of habit. If we optimize for trial volume, we make onboarding too aggressive. Optimizing Activities Logged protects the free base, building the social network effect that feeds the conversion paywalls."
Q: "Won't a 7-day challenge burn out raw beginners?"
Defend: "The challenge accepts *any* activity — walk, yoga, stretch, run. It's about establishing the digital habit of logging with low physical friction. We also track 'Push Opt-out Rates' as a safety valve."
⚔️ Challenging Other Teams
Target Card A (Uber): One-tap cancel rematch
Challenge: "Does matching cancelled riders immediately cannibalize the normal dispatch queue, delaying standard bookings? What safety guardrail metrics prevent standard match SLA degradation?"
Target Card D (Etsy): Camera Visual Search
Challenge: "User-uploaded photos are often dark or poorly angled. A low search precision leads to quick user abandonment. How is 'Empty/Zero Result Rate' or 'Search-to-Detail Click CTR' reflected in your discovery metrics?"
Target Card F (YouTube): Push re-engagement digest
Challenge: "Push notifications to dormant users often backfire, causing users to delete the app. What is your guardrail metric for push-driven App Uninstalls or push disablement rate?"
Bonus Strategy: - Highlight that challenging other teams isn't about being hostile; it's about showcasing systematic product analytics logic. - Focus questions on queue cannibalization (Uber), search precision (Etsy), and spam fatigue (YouTube). - When defending, stand firm on the power of product habits and network density as the ultimate defense.
